Works in Progress

  • The Effects of Initial Placement: Evidence from Economics Job Market Flyouts with Caleb Brobst [slides]
    Abstract

    We study the effect of a good initial placement in the economics job market on candidates’ medium run publication productivity. To isolate causality from selection, we employ two strategies: within-flyout list comparisons in specifications of increasing stringency (as in Greenstone, Hornbeck, and Moretti, 2010), and in the future, quasi-random variation in job postings by field. We currently find a ~30% increase in cumulative 10-year output. Drawing on granular data on flyouts, placement lists, faculty roster snapshots, and individual candidates’ personal websites and résumés, we will explore the mechanisms which account for the success of top institutions.

  • Does Worker Mobility Mediate Knowledge Spillovers? [slides]
    Abstract

    I put to the test the common assumption of knowledge diffusion through encounters between agents. Drawing on granular geo-located data from Revelio Labs on millions of R&D workers in the U.S., I examine and horserace two different channels through which these encounters may occur: physical proximity of workers in separate firms, and the mobility of workers between firms. I model worker mobility between establishments as gravity, as in the trade literature, to capture persistent establishment traits driving inflows and outflows. Work in progress.

Other Writings

  • Search and Competition in the Market for Sick Leaves Master’s thesis, 2024. Advisors: Nicolás Figueroa and Pablo Celhay. [updated version] [references] [original]
    Abstract

    I develop and estimate a model of the market for medical treatment and sick leave, in which patients search imperfectly across physicians who differ in quality and choose how leniently to issue sick leave. Competition for patients leads lower-quality physicians to relax their issuance threshold, producing assortative matching between “dishonest” physicians and patients. I calibrate the model using data from Chile’s national public insurer. In a counterfactual, a 64% increase in the fine for issuing sick leave reduces aggregate sick leaves by 32%, concentrated on the patients with the least medical need.
